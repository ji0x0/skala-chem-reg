Trichloroethylene (TCE); Regulation Under the Toxic Substances 
Control Act (TSCA); Compliance Date Extensions

AGENCY: Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).

ACTION: Proposed rule.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA or Agency) is 
proposing to extend certain compliance dates applicable to certain 
entities subject to the regulation of trichloroethylene (TCE) under the 
Toxic Substances Control Act (TSCA) until December 31, 2027, including 
for certain chemical processors in meeting the prohibition compliance 
deadline for the processing of TCE, industrial and commercial use of 
TCE as a processing aid, and associated wastewater disposal; and for 
refrigerant manufacturers in meeting the compliance deadline for 
phasing down

[[Page 65149]]

TCE feedstock in the production of HFC-134a. This proposed compliance 
extension would allow for necessary and reasonable transition periods 
for regulated entities under TSCA section 6(d).

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

I. Executive Summary

A. Does this action apply to me?

    You may be potentially affected by this rule if you manufacture, 
process, distribute in commerce, use, or dispose of TCE, or products 
containing TCE. TSCA section 3(9) defines the term ``manufacture'' to 
mean ``to import into customs territory of the United States (as 
defined in general note 2 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the 
United States), produce, or manufacture.'' Therefore, unless expressly 
stated otherwise, importers of TCE are subject to provisions regulating 
manufacture of TCE once the chemical enters the customs territory of 
the United States. Potentially affected entities may be included in the 
following lists of North American Industrial Classification System 
(NAICS) codes:
     Wood Window and Door Manufacturing (NAICS code 321911);
     Other Basic Inorganic Chemical Manufacturing (NAICS code 
325180);
     Medicinal and Botanical Manufacturing (NAICS code 325411);
     All Other Miscellaneous Chemical Product and Preparation 
Manufacturing (NAICS code 325998);
     Rubber and Plastics Hoses and Belting Manufacturing (NAICS 
code 326220);
     Rubber Product Manufacturing for Mechanical Use (NAICS 
code 326291); and
     Storage Battery Manufacturing (NAICS code 335911).

B. What is the agency's authority for taking this action?

    Under TSCA section 6(a) (15 U.S.C. 2605(a)), if EPA determines 
through a TSCA section 6(b) risk evaluation that a chemical substance 
presents an unreasonable risk of injury to health or the environment 
under the conditions of use, EPA must by rule apply one or more 
requirements listed in TSCA section 6(a) to the extent necessary so 
that the chemical substance or mixture no longer presents such risk 
under the conditions of use. EPA must specify compliance dates under 
TSCA section 6(d) (15 U.S.C. 2605(d)) for any TSCA section 6(a) rule. 
For TCE, EPA promulgated a final risk management rule under TSCA 
section 6(a) in 2024 (``2024 TCE Final Rule'') (Ref. 1) and an interim 
final rule extending compliance dates for two specific TCE conditions 
of use in 2025 (``2025 TCE Interim Final Rule'') (Ref. 2). Each of 
these TSCA section 6(a) actions include compliance dates pursuant to 
TSCA section 6(d). This action proposes to alter some of the compliance 
dates finalized in 2024 or finalized in subsequent amended rulemakings. 
Unless provided otherwise by law, agencies may change existing 
positions (e.g., reconsider, revise, or rescind prior rules) provided 
that they acknowledge the change in position, offer a reasoned 
explanation for the change, and take any serious reliance interests 
into account. See, e.g., FDA v. Wages & White Lion Invs., L.L.C., 145 
S. Ct. 898, 917 (2025); Encino Motorcars v. Navarro, 579 U.S. 211, 221 
(2016); FCC v. Fox Television Stations, Inc., 556 U.S. 502, 515 (2009); 
Motor Vehicle Mfrs. Ass'n v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., 463 U.S. 
29 (1983). For the reasons set out in Units II and III of this 
preamble, EPA is proposing to revise select compliance dates. EPA is 
not aware of any serious reliance interests regarding this proposal.

C. What action is the agency taking?

    EPA is proposing to amend 40 CFR 751.305 and 40 CFR 751.313 to 
extend the prohibition compliance dates for processing TCE as a 
reactant/intermediate, processing for and industrial and commercial use 
of TCE as a processing aid in: process solvent used in battery 
manufacture, process solvent used in polymer fiber spinning, 
fluoroelastomer manufacture and Alcantara manufacture, extraction 
solvent used in caprolactam manufacture, precipitant used in beta-
cyclodextrin manufacture (hereinafter referred to as ``industrial and 
commercial use of TCE as a processing aid''), and the disposal of TCE 
from such processing or uses to industrial pre-treatment, industrial 
treatment, or publicly owned treatment works from December 18, 2026, to 
December 31, 2027.
    EPA is also proposing to amend 40 CFR 751.307 to extend the 
compliance timeframe for a step in the phase-out of processing TCE as 
an intermediate to manufacture HFC-134a. Specifically, EPA is proposing 
to extend the compliance deadline from June 7, 2027, to December 31, 
2027, for each manufacturer of HFC-134a via processing TCE as an 
intermediate to process TCE at an annual volume no greater than 75 
percent of the baseline.
    EPA is also proposing to amend 40 CFR 751.321 to amend the 
compliance deadline for downstream notification, and the text required 
to be present in the SDS, to accurately reflect the new prohibition 
compliance deadline for processing TCE as a reactant/intermediate, 
processing for and industrial and commercial use of TCE as a processing 
aid, and the disposal of TCE from such processing or uses to industrial 
pre-treatment, industrial treatment, or publicly owned treatment works. 
The new text would be required to be updated 30 days after the 
publication of the final rule.

D. Why is the Agency taking this action?

    EPA is proposing to address ongoing difficulties associated with 
compliance dates for the 2024 TCE Final Rule and 2025 Interim Final 
Rule for certain chemical processors and refrigerant manufacturers due 
to challenges with the implementation of the respective prohibition and 
phaseout, in order to provide a reasonable transition period to no 
longer process TCE. As detailed further in Unit III.A.1, soon after 
publication of the 2024 TCE Final Rule, chemical processors and 
chemical manufacturers explained to EPA that the prohibition on 
processing TCE as a

[[Page 65150]]

reactant/intermediate, and associated wastewater disposal, would cause 
significant hardships to the chemical manufacturing sector because 
byproduct TCE at levels above the regulatory threshold is regularly 
transferred between sites during the manufacture of other chemicals 
(Refs. 3, 4 and 5).
    EPA is also concerned about the prohibition on the processing for 
and industrial and commercial use of TCE as a processing aid with the 
same compliance date, as EPA believes it may also warrant a more 
reasonable compliance timeframe for chemical processing. Due to the 
regular transfer of byproduct TCE between sites during the manufacture 
of other chlorinated solvents, a prohibition on processing TCE on 
December 18, 2026, could have significant negative impacts on the 
production of other chlorinated solvents in the United States (Ref. 3).
    Separately, after publication of the 2024 TCE Final Rule, EPA 
received information from processors of TCE who manufacture HFC-134a 
about the continued need for high production of the refrigerant past 
the 2024 Final Rule's phaseout compliance dates (Ref. 6). As discussed 
further in Unit III.A.2, HFC-134a manufacturers now need a longer 
transition period so they can continue using a higher volume of TCE 
feedstock in order to meet demand for national security and critical 
infrastructure (Ref. 6). EPA's proposed amendment to the compliance 
deadline for downstream notification, and the text required to be 
present in SDSs, is being modified to accurately reflect the new 
prohibition compliance deadlines for processing being proposed in this 
action.

E. What are the incremental economic impacts?

    EPA evaluated the potential incremental economic impacts and 
determined that these changes would have minimal impacts on the 
estimated costs and benefits of the existing actions and would 
primarily result in a delay when those costs and benefits begin 
accruing. Quantified costs are expected to be the same as estimated in 
the 2024 TCE Final Rule but will not be incurred until the proposed 
compliance date extensions expire. Relying on the cost estimates in 
section 7 of the economic analysis of the TCE Final Rule (Ref. 7), EPA 
estimates that the proposed extension of compliance dates would result 
in present value cost savings of $0.62-0.65 million (2024$ in 2026), 
forgone health benefits of $3,795 to $7,844 (2025$), and annualized 
cost savings of $ 0.04-0.06 million (2024$ in 2026, 3%-7% discount rate 
respectively, 20-year time horizon). EPA notes that, in the event that 
alternative compliance timeframes are finalized in response to comments 
received on this proposal, these impacts would be modified accordingly 
(e.g., cost savings likely increase as the duration of the compliance 
timeframe extension increases).

II. Background

    On December 17, 2024, EPA published the 2024 TCE Final Rule under 
TSCA section 6(a) to address unreasonable risk to human health (89 FR 
102568) (FRL-8317-02-OCSPP). The 2024 TCE final rule prohibits the 
manufacture (including import), processing, and distribution in 
commerce of TCE for all uses (industrial, commercial, and consumer), 
while including longer compliance timeframes with reasonable worker 
protections for some processing and industrial and commercial uses 
until the prohibitions come into effect, so that TCE no longer presents 
an unreasonable risk to workers, occupational non-users, consumers, and 
bystanders.
    After publication of the 2024 TCE Final Rule, EPA was made aware of 
a previously unidentified use of TCE as a processing aid in the 
manufacture of nuclear fuel and promulgated an interim final rule in 
September of 2025 to amend the prohibition compliance deadlines until 
September 15, 2028, to provide a reasonable transition period for that 
use (Ref. 2). EPA additionally extended the deadlines for disposal of 
TCE to wastewater for processors of TCE as a reactant/intermediate and 
certain processors and industrial and commercial users of TCE as a 
processing aid to December 18, 2026, to match the prohibition 
compliance date for these processing conditions of use.
    EPA received three TSCA section 21 petitions concerning the 2024 
TCE Final Rule: from American Chemistry Council (ACC) on May 29, 2025 
(withdrawn September 19, 2025) (Refs. 3 and 8); from PPG Industries, 
Inc. on March 24, 2025 (withdrawn June 11, 2025) (Refs. 4 and 9); and 
from the Alliance for a Strong U.S. Battery Sector and Microporous on 
April 30, 2025 (withdrawn July 25, 2025) (Refs. 5 and 10). While these 
petitions were withdrawn, EPA recognizes the potential merits of 
certain requests contained in these petitions and is planning to 
address them in amendments to the rule through a future notice-and-
comment rulemaking.
    After issuing the TCE Final Rule, EPA received petitions for review 
that were consolidated in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third 
Circuit. EPA has since announced its intention to reconsider the TCE 
Final Rule.

III. Provisions of This Proposed Rule

    For any TSCA section 6(a) rule, EPA must specify mandatory 
compliance dates that are ``as soon as practicable,'' while allowing 
for ``a reasonable transition period.'' 15 U.S.C. 2605(d)(1)(B) and 
(E). EPA acknowledges that it is proposing a change in position from 
the 2024 TCE Final Rule and the 2025 TCE Interim Final Rule. EPA 
intended that the compliance timeframes in these rules would be as soon 
as practicable while allowing for a reasonable transition period (see 
Unit I.E of the 2024 TCE Final Rule, and Unit III of the 2025 TCE 
Interim Final Rule). However, the Agency did not fully anticipate the 
challenges that certain entities are now experiencing: for chemical 
processors in meeting the prohibition compliance deadline for the 
processing of TCE, industrial and commercial use of TCE as a processing 
aid, and associated wastewater disposal (Unit III.A); and for 
refrigerant manufacturers in meeting the compliance deadline for 
phasing down TCE feedstock in the production of HFC-134a (Unit III.B). 
Accordingly, EPA proposes that the compliance dates detailed in this 
Unit would be more feasible and workable, and compliant with EPA's 
obligations under TSCA section 6(d) for TCE, than those finalized in 
2024 or amended in 2025.

A. Processing TCE as a Reactant/Intermediate, Industrial and Commercial 
Use of TCE as a Processing Aid, and Their Associated Wastewater 
Disposal

    EPA is proposing to amend the compliance deadline for the 
prohibition on processing TCE as a reactant/intermediate and the 
industrial and commercial use of TCE as a processing aid in certain 
chemical manufacturing to December 31, 2027. EPA is also proposing to 
amend the compliance deadline for the prohibition on the associated 
disposal of TCE to wastewater by processors of TCE and processors and 
industrial and commercial users of TCE as a processing aid to December 
31, 2027.
    In the 2024 TCE Final Rule, EPA finalized a prohibition on 
processing and distributing in commerce (including making available) of 
TCE as of June 16, 2025 (40 CFR 751.305(b)(2)). EPA also finalized 
extended phaseouts for processing of TCE as a reactant/intermediate and 
processing for and the industrial and commercial use of TCE as a 
processing aid to continue until December 2026 (40 CFR

[[Page 65151]]

751.305(b)(9)(i)), and an exclusion from all prohibitions and 
restrictions for processing (including reuse) of TCE as a byproduct 
when that TCE byproduct is processed within a site-limited, physically 
enclosed system that is part of the same overall manufacturing process 
from which the byproduct TCE was generated (40 CFR 751.301(c)).
    After the publication of the 2024 TCE Final Rule, EPA received a 
Section 21 petition from the ACC regarding the exclusion on byproduct 
reprocessing and the wastewater disposal associated with processing TCE 
(Ref. 3). In response, in the 2025 TCE Interim Final Rule EPA amended 
the compliance deadline from September 15, 2025, to December 18, 2026, 
for the prohibition on disposal of TCE to wastewater for processors of 
TCE as a reactant/intermediate and processors for and industrial and 
commercial users of TCE as a processing aid for certain industrial 
sectors. This action aligned the disposal prohibition with the December 
18, 2026, prohibition on such uses in the 2024 Final Rule. This change 
responded to the concerns raised to EPA that processing conditions of 
use also involve disposal of TCE to wastewater that inadvertently was 
not similarly subject to the same compliance deadline (Ref. 2).
    Through the Section 21 Petition and subsequent engagement, EPA was 
made aware after the 2024 final rule that byproduct TCE that is present 
in feedstock streams is sent by railcar between facilities to be 
further processed. This usually occurs between facilities of the same 
company, where one site has chemical manufacturing capabilities that 
another does not (Refs. 3, 8, 11 and 13). If the prohibition on 
processing TCE goes into effect on December 18, 2026, then the 
processing of byproduct TCE that travels between sites will be 
prohibited, along with disposal of this byproduct TCE to wastewater. 
Disposal of this byproduct TCE would therefore require incineration; if 
all byproduct TCE that is currently reused in a different facility must 
be incinerated, it would cause significant implementation concerns, 
including exceeded permit limitations and potential facility closures. 
Petitioners have indicated that the inability to send unpurified 
chlorinated solvent product streams with byproduct TCE between 
facilities would significantly disrupt the national supply chain by 
disproportionately impacting operations in the manufacture of other 
chlorinated solvents which unintentionally produce TCE as a byproduct 
(importantly perchloroethylene, carbon tetrachloride, methylene 
chloride, and 1,2-dichloroethane) in the United States (Refs. 3, 8, 11 
and 13). In the 2024 TCE Final Rule and 2025 TCE Interim Final Rule EPA 
did not anticipate these implications.
    As such, EPA is proposing to amend the compliance deadline for the 
prohibition on processing TCE as a reactant/intermediate and the 
wastewater disposal associated with such processing TCE until December 
31, 2027, so that regulated entities would not be held in non-
compliance with the relevant requirements in the 2024 TCE Final Rule 
and the 2025 TCE Interim Final Rule. EPA is also proposing to amend the 
compliance deadline for the prohibition on industrial and commercial 
use of TCE as a processing aid because EPA believes, based on ACC's 
comments, that this condition of use also needs a longer transition 
period. These ongoing difficulties (see Unit I.D.2) in complying with 
the prohibitions and restrictions of the TCE final rule, particularly 
the need to transfer byproduct chemicals off site, warrant a longer 
reasonable transition period for this group of uses than EPA expected 
in 2024 or 2025. Given the two years provided for this compliance date 
in the 2024 TCE rule, EPA considers this extension of no more than one 
year to be appropriate because it is ``as soon as practicable,'' while 
allowing for ``a reasonable transition period'' (15 U.S.C. 
2605(d)(1)(B) and (E)). Separately, EPA has initiated a rulemaking 
under RIN 2070-AL41 through which the Agency is reconsidering various 
elements of the 2024 TCE Final Rule, including various workplace 
exposure controls and prohibitions on certain industrial and commercial 
uses.

B. Processing TCE as an Intermediate for the Manufacture of HFC-134a

    EPA is proposing to amend the compliance deadline for the first 
phaseout reduction of the processing of TCE as an intermediate for the 
manufacture of HFC-134a (1,1,1,2-tetrafluroethane; CASRN 811-97-2). 
Under the 2024 TCE Final Rule, stakeholders processing TCE to 
manufacture HFC-134a would have to cut the amount of TCE feedstock they 
use to 75% of their baseline by June 7, 2027. EPA is proposing to amend 
the compliance deadline so that this reduction in TCE feedstock would 
instead occur by December 31, 2027.
    At the time of publication of the 2024 TCE Final Rule, the public 
comments EPA had received from the regulated community supported this 
phaseout schedule. After the publication of the 2024 TCE Final Rule, 
EPA received new information from Orbia Fluor & Energy Materials 
(Orbia) regarding the continued need for HFC-134a for military, 
industrial, and medical applications at rates higher than the 2024 TCE 
final rule phaseout accounted for (Refs. 6 and 12). Since the 
publication of the 2024 TCE Final Rule, the 2024 TCE final rule 
phaseout is no longer in alignment with the AIM Act phasedown because 
Orbia has received more allowances and anticipates receiving further 
allowances under the AIM Act to manufacture HFC-134a than initially 
anticipated (Ref. 6). The demand for HFC-134a from users in critical 
infrastructure and national security applications is now expected to 
remain significant through the early 2030s (Ref. 12).
    By proposing to amend the compliance deadline for the first 
phasedown reduction in TCE feedstock volume to be in December instead 
of June 2027, EPA intends to allow time for a reasonable transition 
period given the changes in AIM Act allowances received for HFC-134a 
manufacture. This new compliance deadline will allow manufacturers of 
HFC-134a to meet needs from critical infrastructure and national 
security users while allowing for a transition that has a reasonable 
timeframe and will not produce unintended negative consequences. It is 
important to amend this compliance date because if the June 2027 date 
is not changed, HFC-134a manufacturers must otherwise begin curtailing 
their production of the refrigerant before the demand abates, 
negatively impacting critical infrastructure and national security 
application needs. Given the two and a half years provided for this 
compliance date in the 2024 TCE rule, EPA considers this extension of 
no more than 6 and a half months to be appropriate because it is ``as 
soon as practicable,'' while allowing for ``a reasonable transition 
period'' (15 U.S.C. 2605(d)(1)(B) and (E)).
    Separately, EPA has initiated a rulemaking under RIN 2070-AL41 
through which the Agency is reconsidering various elements of the 2024 
TCE final rule, including stakeholders' larger issues with the use of 
TCE for HFC-134a manufacturing.

C. Downstream Notification

    In keeping with the proposed new prohibition compliance deadline 
for processing TCE as a reactant/intermediate in chemical 
manufacturing, processing for and industrial and commercial use of TCE 
as a processing aid in certain chemical manufacturing, and the disposal 
of TCE

[[Page 65152]]

from such processing or uses to industrial pre-treatment, industrial 
treatment, or publicly owned treatment works, EPA also proposes to 
amend the downstream notification requirements of the 2024 TCE Final 
Rule to accurately reflect the restrictions on TCE. Accurate downstream 
notification is necessary to spread awareness throughout the supply 
chain of the restrictions on use of TCE under TSCA, as well as provide 
information to commercial end users about allowable uses of TCE until 
the prohibition compliance dates. EPA recognizes that the 2024 TCE 
Final Rule and 2025 Interim Final Rule compliance deadlines for 
updating the SDS of June 16, 2025, and December 16, 2025, respectively, 
have already passed and as such, manufacturers, processors, and 
distributors in commerce of TCE would need to update or amend their SDS 
shortly after doing so already. EPA proposes that 30 days from the 
publication of the final rule is a practicable time to update the SDS 
for manufacturers, processors, and distributors in commerce of TCE.

IV. Requests for Comment

    EPA is requesting public comment on all aspects of this proposal. 
EPA notes that the proposed compliance dates may be subject to change 
depending on the timing of the final rulemaking and public comment.
    1. EPA requests comment on the changes proposed in this action, 
including whether stakeholders have any serious reliance interests that 
may be impacted and, if so, how such interests should be accounted for 
in any final action.
    2. EPA requests comment on a stakeholder requested alternative 
compliance deadline of December 18, 2028, for the TCE uses that this 
rule proposes to extend, and whether there is a basis that this longer 
deadline, compared to the proposed date of December 31, 2027, would be 
``as soon as practicable,'' while allowing for ``a reasonable 
transition period.'' (15 U.S.C. 2605(d)(1)(B) and (E)) (Ref. 13).
    3. EPA requests comment on an expedited (e.g., 14 day) or immediate 
effective date of this extension rule because the December 18, 2026, 
compliance deadline is fast approaching.

D. Regulatory Flexibility Act (RFA)

    I certify that this action will not have a significant economic 
impact on a substantial number of small entities under the RFA, 5 
U.S.C. 601 et seq. In making this determination, EPA concludes that the 
impact of concern for this action is any significant adverse economic 
impact on small entities, and the Agency is certifying that this rule 
will not have a significant economic impact on a substantial number of 
small entities because the rule relieves regulatory burden on the small 
entities subject to the rule. This action would extend the compliance 
date for the prohibition on processing TCE as a reactant/intermediate 
and the industrial and commercial use of TCE as a processing aid and 
their associated disposal until 2028, the compliance date for the first 
phase of the phaseout of TCE processing in the manufacture of HFC-134a 
until 2028, and the compliance deadline for downstream notification and 
the text required to be present in SDSs for TCE starting 30 days after 
publication of the final rule. We have therefore concluded that this 
action will relieve regulatory burden for all directly regulated small 
entities.

E. Unfunded Mandates Reform Act (UMRA)

    This action does not contain an unfunded mandate as described in 
UMRA, 2 U.S.C. 1531-1538, and does not significantly or uniquely affect 
small governments. The costs involved in this action are estimated not 
to exceed $187 million in 2024$ ($100 million in 1995$, adjusted for 
inflation using the GDP implicit price deflator) or more in any one 
year. EPA estimates the annualized cost savings of extending compliance 
dates in this action to be $0.04-0.06 million.

F. Executive Order 13132: Federalism

    This action does not have federalism implications as specified in 
Executive Order 13132 (64 FR 43255, August 10, 1999) because it will 
not have substantial direct effects on the States, on the relationship 
between the national government and the States, or on the distribution 
of power and responsibilities among the various levels of government.

G. Executive Order 13175: Consultation and Coordination With Indian 
Tribal Governments

    This action does not have Tribal implications as specified in 
Executive Order 13175 (65 FR 67249, November 9, 2000) because it does 
not have substantial direct effects on one or more Indian Tribes, on 
the relationship between the Federal Government and Indian Tribes, or 
on the distribution of power and responsibilities between the Federal 
Government and Indian Tribes. Thus, Executive Order 13175 does not 
apply to this action.

H. Executive Order 13045: Protection of Children From Environmental 
Health Risks and Safety Risks

    Executive Order 13045 (62 FR 19885, April 23, 1997) directs federal 
agencies to include an evaluation of the health and safety effects of 
the planned regulation on children in federal health and safety 
standards and explain why the regulation is preferable to potentially 
effective and reasonably feasible alternatives. This action is not 
subject to Executive Order 13045 because it is not a significant 
regulatory action under section 3(f)(1) of Executive Order 12866, and 
because the EPA does not believe the environmental health or safety 
risks addressed by this action present a disproportionate risk to 
children.
    Nevertheless, as in the 2024 TCE final rule, EPA's Policy on 
Children's Health applies to this action. The environmental health or 
safety risks addressed by the 2024 TCE final rule present a 
disproportionate risk to children due to TCE's developmental toxicity, 
as described in Unit VIII.G of that rule. However, this action would 
not alter the requirements of the final rule promulgated in 2024 that 
addresses the unreasonable risks presented by TCE under its conditions 
of use. Rather, this action would delay when entities must comply with 
certain provisions in order to provide a reasonable transition period 
for compliance as required under TSCA section 6(d)(1)(E). Information 
on how the Policy was applied in the 2024 TCE final rule and on that 
action's health and risk assessments is available under Units II.C., 
II.D. and IV.A of the final rule as well as in the 2020 Risk 
Evaluation, and the Economic Analysis for the final rule.

I. Executive Order 13211: Actions Concerning Regulations That 
Significantly Affect Energy Supply, Distribution, or Use

    This action is not a ``significant energy action'' as defined in 
Executive Order 13211 (66 FR 28355, May 22, 2001) because it is not 
likely to have a significant adverse effect on the supply, distribution 
or use of energy.

J. National Technology Transfer and Advancement Act (NTTAA)

    This action does not involve technical standards under NTTAA 
section 12(d), 15 U.S.C. 272. EPA determined that the 2024 TCE final 
rule involves environmental monitoring or measurement for occupational 
inhalation exposures and, consistent with the Agency's Performance 
Based Measurement System, decided not to require the use of specific, 
prescribed analytic methods. However, this action would simply extend 
compliance dates as described in Unit III.
